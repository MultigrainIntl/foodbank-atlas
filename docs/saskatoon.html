Food-need by neighbourhood — Saskatoon Food Bank & Learning Centre 🇨🇦 Canada

Every dissemination area in Saskatoon (CMA), Saskatchewan (about 400–700 people each — finer than a U.S. census tract), shaded by food need — a composite of the low-income measure (LIM-AT), reliance on government transfers, and income. Darker = higher need. Click any neighbourhood for its numbers. Three views: use the toggle just below to switch between overall food need, the low-income rate (LIM-AT), and the Indigenous layer (residents reporting Indigenous identity — a population standard maps often under-represent).

How to use it

Neighbourhood, not city. This splits the region's food-insecurity picture into 449 dissemination areas so distributions, mobile pantries, and the right product mix can be aimed where need concentrates.

Consistent everywhere. Every food bank's map is generated by the same pipeline — U.S. food banks on Census data, Canadian food banks on Statistics Canada data — so any two are directly comparable.

Statistics Canada 2021 Census of Population (dissemination area) via the keyless Census Profile SDMX web service · boundaries from StatCan open boundary files · Food-need score = min-max composite of LIM-AT prevalence, government-transfer share of income, and inverse median household income across the region's scorable dissemination areas. Illustrative — not a Statistics Canada or Food Banks Canada product.
