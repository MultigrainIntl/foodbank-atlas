Food-need by neighborhood — Alameda County Community Food Bank

Every census tract in Alameda County, California (about 4,000 people each), shaded by food need — a composite of poverty, share under 200% of the poverty line, SNAP receipt, and low income. Darker = higher need. Tracts outlined in purple are student / group-quarters areas (campuses, military housing, facilities), kept in rather than filtered out. Click any neighborhood for its numbers. Three views: use the toggle just below to switch between overall food need, the SNAP gap (people eligible for SNAP but likely not enrolled), and the Indigenous layer (American Indian / Alaska Native residents, a population standard maps often erase).

How to use it

Neighborhood, not county. This splits the county's food-insecurity figure into 377 neighborhoods so distributions, mobile pantries, and the right product mix can be aimed where need concentrates.

Consistent everywhere. Every food bank's map is generated by the same pipeline from U.S. Census data — same method, same look, same scoring — so any two are directly comparable.

U.S. Census ACS 5-year (tract) via Census Reporter · place names via the U.S. Census geocoder · Food-need score = min-max composite of poverty, <200% FPL, SNAP, and inverse median income across the region's residential tracts. Illustrative — not a Feeding America product.
