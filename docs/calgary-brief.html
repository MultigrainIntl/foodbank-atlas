Calgary (CMA), Alberta · generated September 1, 2026

Across Calgary (CMA), Alberta, the Food-Need Atlas scores every one of 1,898 dissemination areas (neighbourhoods of ~400–700 people) on the low-income measure (LIM-AT), reliance on government transfers, and income. 13 neighbourhoods — about 7,904 people — register high food need, and are where distribution, mobile pantries and the right product mix return the most.

Indigenous food insecurity: about 45,640 residents across Calgary (CMA), Alberta report Indigenous identity. Standard neighbourhood maps under-represent Indigenous need — some reserves are incompletely enumerated — so treat this as a floor and route support through Indigenous-led programs and food-sovereignty initiatives.

Funding to pursue

Federal & national programs (confirm current eligibility before applying):

  • Local Food Infrastructure Fund (AAFC) — Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada grants for community organizations to buy equipment and infrastructure — refrigeration, trucks, storage — that expands local food access. Built for exactly the kind of capacity a food bank needs to move more food.
  • Nutrition North Canada — Retail subsidy that lowers the cost of nutritious food in isolated northern communities. The backbone federal program for the North, where food costs and food insecurity are highest.
  • Nutrition North — Harvesters Support Grant — Funds Indigenous harvesting of traditional and country foods (hunting, fishing, gathering) in eligible communities — support on communities' own terms, and the closest Canadian analog to the U.S. FDPIR tribal food program. For northern and remote First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities, not urban centres.
  • Food Banks Canada — The national network — national food sourcing and sharing, disaster response, and grant programs its member food banks can access. Membership plugs a food bank into national purchasing and support.
  • Reaching Home (ESDC) — Canada's homelessness-strategy funding, delivered through community entities. Some food banks and their partners tap it for food-and-shelter programming that reaches the same households.

Food Aid Project can also source and ship a mixed truckload of shelf-stable staples into these neighbourhoods.
